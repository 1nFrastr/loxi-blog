In offline cashier scenarios such as supermarkets and chain restaurants, merchants often need:

- To distinguish different checkout devices — user-scanned payment QR codes, POS terminals, barcode scanners, WeChat palm-pay devices, and so on
- To distinguish orders by counter — large stores often have multiple checkout lanes
- To break down sales by cashier at the same counter for attendance or performance

Online e-commerce payment logic usually stops at store level: the user pays under a store. That granularity is not fine enough for offline merchants’ operational needs.

So offline cashier systems introduce the **terminal** concept.

**A terminal is an abstraction — not a physical entity, but a hub that connects multiple entities.**

A terminal can provide:

1. Pluggable binding of different payment device types
2. Pluggable shared devices such as receipt printers and cloud speakers
3. Unlimited merchant-defined terminals for different reconciliation needs and finer operations
